## Problem Statement

The book is structurally complete but operationally a long first draft. It runs ~33,000 words — novella length — when adult literary folk horror that is taken seriously lives at 65–85k. The back half compresses scenes into summary, several outline promises go unkept, and Act Three's timeline is scrambled. The author wants to expand and intensify the book to a releasable, serious-literary state.

## Goals

1. **Expand to ~70,000 words** by converting summary into scene, not by padding.
2. **Make the danger feel real** — readers should feel at risk, and death should occur on the page — while preserving the restrained, literary folk-horror register. (Deepen dread by craft; do not go overt/visceral.)
3. **Fix the Act Three timeline tangle** (Ch 15–17 currently overlap in time).
4. **Deliver the broken promises** the outline set up and the draft dropped.
5. **De-risk the cultural material** by fictionalizing real institutions while keeping the people recognizably Ojibwe, writing to maximum restraint, and flagging passages for an optional later sensitivity read.
6. Keep everything the draft already does well (see *What NOT to Touch*).

## Locked Decisions

| Decision | Value |
|---|---|
| Horror register | Literary restraint preserved; **mortal stakes real** (deaths on the page, genuine peril). Not overt/visceral horror. |
| Scope | **Hybrid** — expand every chapter to scene-depth + fix timeline + deliver payoffs + restore 2–3 cut beats that raise stakes. |
| Word target | ~70,000 (floor 65k). |
| Naming | Fictionalize the real band name and the town of Watersmeet; keep characters recognizably Ojibwe. |
| Sensitivity reader | Deferred. Write reader-ready with flagged passages; author may commission a reader before release. |
| Author name | Pen name **Seven Martin** (replace "Shane Martin" on the title page). |
| Execution | Blueprint-first, then front-to-back prose. |

## 1. New Chapter Structure (22 → ~26 chapters)

The current Act Three is scrambled: Ch 15 runs Saturday→Wednesday-dawn, Ch 16 jumps back to Sunday/Tuesday for the family visits, Ch 17 is Tuesday→Wednesday again. The revision rebuilds the back half as a **strictly chronological day-by-day countdown** (Saturday forecast → Wednesday ceremony) in which the escalating haunted nights and the family-rallying visits interleave in real time.

**Acts One & Two (Ch 1–14): expand in place**, following the diagnostic's per-chapter targets. No reordering. Net growth ~8k→~10k (Act One) and ~14k→~22k (Act Two).

**Act Three — the Countdown (chronological rebuild).** Proposed beat order (chapter breaks to be finalized in the implementation plan):

1. **Spring Coming / the thaw.** Forecast, declining-ice ledger pattern, the watch now unkept. First all-night knocking. (From current Ch 15 opening.)
2. **The families, by day.** Lehtinen visit, then Saari visit (Erik's reluctant "what time"), interleaved chronologically with the nights — not a flashback block. Restore the **two-factions** tension. (From current Ch 16, re-timed.)
3. **The snowmobiler — dramatized.** Dale Numminen's death brought close: Aino and Mikko at the inlet as the machine is recovered. First on-page modern kill. (Restored from outline Ch 17.)
4. **The families convene (private).** Small, the night before — the three families aligning on what they're agreeing to. NOT the Finnish-Hall Town Meeting (too on-the-nose). (Restored, miniaturized, from outline Ch 21.)
5. **The Saari chainsaw death — "The Lid Comes Off."** A Saari who rejects the Wednesday plan goes to the ice to force his own ending and is taken. On-page death, the climax-window stakes spike. Must be a **different Saari than Erik** (a relative — e.g. brother/cousin/son — who is the "end-it-now" faction). (Restored from outline Ch 20.)
6. **The Last Night.** The worst night, in the house. Expanded to scene-depth, hour by hour. (Current Ch 17, grown.)
7. **The Morning.** Gathering the documents at dawn, the cold sauna, the snow pushed back in a radius. (Split from current Ch 17.)

**Climax & Resolution (renumbered):**

8. **The Ceremony** (current Ch 18) — intensified Aino-peril; **the Finnish-prayer payoff** lands here.
9. **Burning** (current Ch 19, retitled from "What Her Father Wrote at the End") — the sauna burns; Aino cries for each in turn.
10. **Coals and Stone** (current Ch 20, retitled from the leftover "The Lid Comes Off") — falling action; **the mother's call** ("I shouldn't have brought you back") lands here or in After.
11. **After** (current Ch 21) — the week of estate-clearing, with restored external-world texture (a death, a closing, the town moving on).
12. **She Does Not Get Far** (current Ch 22) — the coda, expanded; the new lake registers as presence; "I can hear it now."

Final chapter count and exact breaks resolved in the implementation plan; target ~26.

## 2. Mortal-Stakes Design ("feel at risk")

All within the lore's constraint (*she cannot kill at will; deaths happen because those people came to her*):

- **Toivo's 1962 drowning** — short ledger-driven embedded flashback in Ch 8. Death on the page early; closes the book's biggest historical hole.
- **Dale the snowmobiler** — moved from offscreen phone call to a witnessed recovery at the inlet.
- **The Saari chainsaw death** — fully on-page; the reader sees the lake take someone *now*.
- **Aino's ceremony peril** — intensify the near-pull-under so the outcome feels genuinely uncertain and the rescue costs something.
- **In-house haunting** — cold, counting knocks, the blow on the door, the cold migrating into the walls/ceiling — expanded to full scene-depth (the book's strongest dread engine).

## 3. Payoff Ledger (deliver or deliberately resolve)

| Promise | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Toivo's drowning | Dramatized, Ch 8 (see §2). |
| Finnish folklore (*väki*, *vetehinen*) | Restored in the Helmi chapter (Ch 11), restrained, broken out of monologue. |
| Aino's unconscious Finnish at climax | One phrase she didn't know she still knew, at the ceremony (e.g. *anna sen mennä* said back to the lake, or *anna meidän anteeksi* — forgive us). |
| Mother's call | "I shouldn't have brought you back" restored in resolution. |
| Carved marks | Quiet partial payoff (the men's containment-marks — acknowledgment without restitution made literal); never fully decoded; burn in the fire. |
| Basement tools | Tied to the watch (carving/maintaining the sauna and the marks); small payoff. |
| Lake's presence Ch 11–14 | Restored — it goes quiet during the investigation arc and shouldn't. |

## 4. The Finnish-Language Thread

Tuned across the whole front-to-back pass. Early: Finnish words read as intrusions, translated away. Late: English becomes insufficient; by the climax she prays in Finnish without deciding to. Currently used twice (Ch 4) and dropped — this is the spine of "her armor slipping" and must run the length of the book.

## 5. Naming & Ethics

- Replace **"Lac Vieux Desert Band of Lake Superior Chippewa"** with an **invented band** of Lake Superior Ojibwe (working name TBD in implementation, e.g. a band tied to the fictional geography).
- Fictionalize **Watersmeet** into an invented town (the elder/Louise live there).
- Characters remain recognizably **Ojibwe** — specificity is respect; a wholly-invented people would read as erasure and gut the book's historical grounding.
- **Maximum restraint** held throughout: the elder refuses to translate; what Mindemoyen became is never named or systematized; the ceremony's interior content is never depicted; the Ojibwe characters keep full agency and are never the threat. The horror belongs to the four Finnish families.
- **Flagged-passage protocol:** every passage touching Ojibwe culture, the ceremony, the elder's dialogue, and the name "Mindemoyen" gets an inline review marker so an optional later sensitivity-reader pass is turnkey. Markers collected into a reader brief at the end.

## 6. What NOT to Touch

(Per diagnostic — preserve.)

- Ch 1 opening pages (radiator → Subaru → highway).
- The hatch scene, Ch 7.
- The three-knocks scene, Ch 10 (small additions only).
- The father's letter, Ch 5.
- The burning of the sauna and Aino crying for each family member in turn, Ch 19.
- The closing "I can hear it now" beat, Ch 22.
- **The voice.** The dryness, the laconic interior, thinking-in-objects. Do not sand it.

## 7. Working Process

1. **Spec** (this document) — approved.
2. **Implementation plan** (writing-plans) — chapter-by-chapter task breakdown with per-chapter word targets, the finalized chapter map/numbering, and the payoff/flag checklist.
3. **Execute front-to-back.** Author reviews in act-sized batches; iterate per batch.
4. **Rebuild deliverables** — regenerate the KDP ebook HTML and print DOCX from the revised text; update the title page to **Seven Martin**.
5. **Optional sensitivity read** before release, using the collected reader brief.

## Open Items (resolve during implementation)

- Final chapter count and exact chapter breaks (~26).
- Invented band name and invented town name.
- Whether the mother's call lands in "Coals and Stone" or "After."
- Whether to keep the name "Mindemoyen" (flagged for reader); decide before release.
- Disposition of the father's 42 ledgers in-story (kept ambiguous in current draft — preserve or resolve).

## Non-Goals

- No overt/visceral-horror rewrite; the restrained register is retained.
- No change to POV (close third, present tense) or to the core plot architecture.
- No restoration of the on-the-nose Finnish-Hall Town Meeting (replaced by the small private convening).
- Cover design, blurb, and KDP metadata are out of scope for this revision (separate task).
