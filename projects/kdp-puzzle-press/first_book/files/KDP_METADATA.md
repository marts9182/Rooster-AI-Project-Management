## Description (Amazon book page — KDP accepts limited HTML: `<b> <i> <br>`)

```html
<b>Some things the cold keeps. Some things it gives back.</b><br><br>
For nineteen years, Aino Hietala stayed away. When her estranged father dies alone in the snowbound Upper Peninsula town of Kivijärvi, she drives north to bury him and empty the house she grew up in. A week, she tells herself. In and out.<br><br>
But the lake behind the house is wrong. Footprints cross the snow each night and end at the foundation. A knocking comes up through the floor, three at a time, keeping perfect time. And out over the ice stands a sauna her father built and tended alone, every week of his life — alongside ledgers, going back to 1919, that record in a careful hand what four Finnish men did to the woman who would not leave her land, and what they built to keep her down.<br><br>
The watch her family kept for a hundred years is broken. The thaw is coming early. And whatever has waited beneath the ice is done waiting.<br><br>
<i>Anna Sen Mennä</i> — Finnish for <i>let it go</i> — is a quiet, devastating debut of literary folk horror about inheritance, buried guilt, and the cost of finally speaking a name aloud. For readers of Jennifer McMahon's <i>The Winter People</i>, Andrew Michael Hurley's <i>The Loney</i>, and Catriona Ward.
```
